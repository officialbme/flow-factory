A gutter guard is a cover or mesh that lets water in while keeping debris out, so your gutters stop becoming a gutter-shaped planter every fall. Across the OKC metro the usual culprits are oak and cottonwood leaves, cottonwood fluff in early summer, pine needles, and the grit that washes off aging shingles — all of which pack down into a clog that sends water over the edge and against your foundation.

We install leaf filter and micro-mesh guard systems on both brand-new gutters and the ones already on your house. The right guard depends on your tree cover and roof: a fine micro-mesh stops pine needles and shingle grit, while a sturdier perforated guard sheds big oak leaves and holds up under ice. We will tell you honestly which one your home actually needs.

What you get

Done the right way

  • Fits new or existing gutters. No need to replace good gutters — most guard systems retrofit onto what you already have.
  • Stops the real OKC offenders. Oak and cottonwood leaves, pine needles, cottonwood fluff, and shingle grit — the stuff that clogs metro gutters.
  • Micro-mesh or perforated. Matched to your tree cover so you get the right balance of fine filtration and flow.
  • Fewer ladders, less risk. Cut the twice-a-year cleaning down to the occasional rinse — and stay off the roof in the process.
  • Protects the whole system. Debris-free gutters mean no overflow against fascia, foundation, or landscaping.
  • Clean, flush fit. Installed to sit tight under the shingle line — it protects, it does not advertise.

Good to know

Common questions

Do gutter guards really work?

A quality guard, installed correctly and sized to your trees, dramatically cuts clogs and overflow. No guard is "never clean again," but a good leaf filter system turns twice-a-year ladder work into an occasional rinse.

Can you put guards on my existing gutters?

Usually, yes — most guard and leaf filter systems retrofit onto existing gutters as long as they are sound and pitched right. If your gutters are sagging or undersized, we will tell you before adding guards.

Which gutter guard is best for Oklahoma?

It depends on your trees. Fine micro-mesh is best against pine needles and shingle grit; a stronger perforated guard sheds big oak and cottonwood leaves and handles ice better. We match the guard to your roof.

Will guards stop ice dams?

Guards help keep debris from trapping water, but ice dams come from attic heat loss. We can point you in the right direction, but guards alone are not an ice-dam cure.
